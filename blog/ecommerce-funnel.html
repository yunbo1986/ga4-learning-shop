Reading a Simple Ecommerce Funnel in GA4

A funnel shows where shoppers move forward and where they leave the journey.

Begin with four useful steps

Your demo shop has a natural sequence: view_item, add_to_cart, begin_checkout, and purchase. Each step represents stronger purchase intent.

Example: 100 product viewers → 40 carts → 20 checkouts → 10 purchases. The step-to-step rates are 40%, 50%, and 50%.

Look for the largest drop

The largest loss often points to the first place worth investigating. A low cart rate may mean weak product information; a low checkout completion rate may suggest confusion, cost, or trust concerns.

Compare meaningful groups

Compare device types, traffic sources, new and returning users, or product categories. A total conversion rate can hide a strong segment and a struggling segment.

Use data to ask better questions

A funnel identifies where behavior changes, but it does not prove why. Combine the numbers with page review, user feedback, and careful experiments before making decisions.
